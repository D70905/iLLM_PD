# NCAT as-built versus iLLM-PD candidate real-FEA benchmark

Date: 2026-07-16

## What was run

Seven NCAT Cracking Group as-built structures and seven iLLM-PD candidate alternatives were evaluated with real ABAQUS FEA under the same fixed NCAT material inputs. The iLLM-PD candidates were generated by the frozen-policy surrogate screen, then independently re-evaluated with real FEA.

## Main result

- As-built compliant sections: 0 / 7
- iLLM-PD candidate compliant sections: 0 / 7
- Mean DSR: as-built 0.190, candidate 0.364
- DSR improved in 7 / 7 sections
- Mean relative DSR improvement: 89.8%
- Mean cost change: 1.7% (range -0.3% to 6.3%)
- Predicted rutting response reduced in 7 / 7 sections
- Mean predicted rutting change: -40.3%
- Mean subgrade vertical strain change: -16.9%
- Mean HMA rutting strain proxy change: -26.4%

## Interpretation

The real-FEA benchmark shows a consistent improvement direction, but not completed design compliance. The iLLM-PD candidates improved DSR in every NCAT section and reduced the rutting-related responses, with small average cost change. However, no candidate reached full JTG compliance after real-FEA verification. The remaining controlling failure is asphalt-layer fatigue (B1) for both as-built and candidate designs.

This should be reported as a retrospective design-response benchmark, not as a successful field validation. The strongest defensible claim is that iLLM-PD moved the fixed-material NCAT designs in the correct mechanical direction under real FEA, but the current frozen-policy candidate set did not deliver compliant NCAT alternatives.

## Next step

To turn this into a stronger result, run either:

1. a real-FEA policy rollout with more steps and/or NCAT-specific bounds, or
2. an independent fixed-material feasibility audit to determine whether compliant NCAT designs exist in the allowed thickness space.

If a compliant design exists but the frozen policy does not find it, the result diagnoses policy/search transfer. If no compliant design exists under fixed NCAT materials and bounds, the result diagnoses an input/constraint limitation rather than policy failure.
